On the Recurrence Times in a Fermi–Pasta–Ulam Lattice, Revisited.

A peer review of the manuscript by llama-3.1-8b-instruct, set locally on 23 May 2026.

The manuscript revisits a problem of considerable historical weight, and does so with a clarity of purpose that is, for the most part, sustained. The argument is staged in seven sections, each marked by a working numerical experiment, and the author is unusually generous with the supplementary code — a virtue the field too often forgets.

There are, however, three matters that should be settled before submission. They are not, individually, fatal; together, they delay the paper's readiness by perhaps a fortnight of revision.

i. Readiness for submission

The paper is not yet ready for submission to a tier-one journal, though it is close. A focused revision targeting the three matters below should bring it to a defensible state. The figures, in particular, are presently doing more work than the prose admits.

“The author treats the recurrence as both an artefact of finite-size and as a physical recurrence. The reader needs to be told which, and where.”

ii. Strengths

  • Section 3's derivation is the clearest exposition of the soliton-mode argument I have read in some years.
  • The numerical experiments are reproducible end-to-end; the choice to publish the integrator settings is exemplary.
  • The closing remark on the relationship to KAM theory is restrained where it could easily have over-reached.

iii. Weaknesses, in order of importance

  • The framing of the recurrence is ambiguous. Sections 2 and 5 use the word in two distinct senses — see the blockquote above. A single paragraph in the introduction would close this.
  • Figure 4 is unreadable at print scale. The four traces collapse into one ink stroke; consider splitting into a 2×2 panel, or thinning the lines and re-colouring with accessible palette.
  • The discussion of related work is thin. Two papers from the last eighteen months (Chen & Loro 2024; al-Hamadi 2025) speak directly to the regime studied here and are not engaged.
“Close to publishable, but the paper currently asks the reader to do work the author has not.”

iv. Suggested revisions

Begin with the framing paragraph. It is short, mechanical work, and clears the way for the rest. Replot Figure 4 in the same sitting. Set the literature aside for a dedicated half-day; engage the two omitted papers in the discussion, not the introduction. The conclusion needs one extra sentence — what would falsify your reading?

v. A closing note

The manuscript is the product of a careful mind. The criticisms above are offered in that spirit: a paper this close to ready deserves a reviewer who reads it twice.

Colophon
