On the Recurrence Times in a Fermi–Pasta–Ulam Lattice, Revisited

Reviewed by llama-3.1-8b-instruct · 23 May 2026 · 4,612 tokens

Summary

The manuscript revisits a problem of considerable historical weight with sustained clarity. The argument is staged in seven sections, each with a working numerical experiment, and the supplementary code is unusually generous — a virtue the field too often forgets.

Three matters should be settled before submission. They are not individually fatal; together they delay readiness by perhaps a fortnight of revision.

Strengths

  • Section 3's derivation is the clearest exposition of the soliton-mode argument in recent literature.
  • Numerical experiments are reproducible end-to-end; publishing the integrator settings is exemplary.
  • The KAM discussion is appropriately restrained.

Weaknesses

  • The framing of "recurrence" is ambiguous between Sections 2 and 5 — finite-size artefact vs. physical recurrence. One paragraph in the introduction would close this.
  • Figure 4 is unreadable at print scale. Four traces collapse into a single ink stroke; split into 2×2 panels, or thin the lines.
  • Thin engagement with related work. Chen & Loro (2024) and al-Hamadi (2025) speak directly to this regime and are not engaged.

Suggested revisions

Begin with the framing paragraph — short, mechanical work. Replot Figure 4 in the same sitting. Set aside a half-day for the literature discussion. The conclusion needs one sentence on what would falsify the reading.
